# Statutory basis of Kenyan withholding tax on government-securities interest paid to resident individuals (as of November 2026)

**Primary text read.** These notes come from the full text of the **Income Tax Act (Cap. 470), Kenya Law / Laws.Africa consolidation "Legislation as at 1 July 2026"** (FRBR `/akn/ke/act/1973/16/eng@2026-07-01`). KRA publishes it as `Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf`: https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf (PDF created 2026-09-18). I fetched it through WebFetch, and `pypdf` extracted all 144 pages. Its cover says "outstanding amendments that have not yet been applied: Act No. 19 of 2026". In fact Act No. 19 of 2026 is already cited 36 times in the text, so that note looks stale. To cross-check, I also read the **Finance Act 2026 itself** (Kenya Gazette Supplement No. 157, Acts No. 19, assent 23 June 2026): https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Finance-Act-2026.pdf. For the earlier state of the law I read KRA's **Income Tax Act, Revised Edition 2018 [Rev. 2017]**: https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/IncomeTaxAct2of1975.pdf. Quotations below are verbatim from those extractions. Ligatures are normalised, and the drafting errors are kept as printed.

Confidence: **high** on what the Act says now, since it is primary text. Confidence is **medium-low** on the dates some rules came in, because the consolidation's amendment notes are incomplete before about 2014.

## 1. Third Schedule, Head B: resident withholding rates on interest

### Takeaway
The resident withholding rates are in **Third Schedule, Head B, paragraph 5**. Two subparagraphs deal with interest.

- **Para 5(b)** covers interest generally: 25% on bearer instruments other than Government bearer bonds; 15% on Government bearer bonds of at least two years "and other sources"; and **10% on "bearer bonds with a maturity of ten years and above"**.
- **Para 5(h)** covers **qualifying interest**, which means a resident individual's interest: **10% for housing bonds, 20% for bearer instruments, 15% "in any other case", all final tax**.

The statutory 10%-at-ten-years wording appears only in 5(b)(iii), and it is worded for **bearer** bonds. Para 5(h), the paragraph that governs resident individuals, has **no ten-year rule**. Read literally, it puts a resident individual's interest on a registered, non-bearer Treasury bond of any tenor at 15%. That is a real conflict with market practice (see Inferences).

### Cited findings
- Head B, para 5 (current text, 1 July 2026 consolidation), verbatim:
  > "5. The resident withholding tax rates shall be– … (b) in respect of interest, discount or original issue discount arising from– (i) bearer instrument other than a Government bearer bond of at least two years duration, twenty-five per cent; (ii) Government Bearer Bond of at least two years duration and other sources, fifteen per cent; (iii) bearer bonds with a maturity of ten years and above, ten per cent of the gross amount payable, of the gross amount payable;"

  The doubled "of the gross amount payable" is in the printed text. — [Income Tax Act Cap. 470, as at 1 July 2026 (Kenya Law, via KRA)](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)
- Head B, para 5(h), verbatim:
  > "(h) in respect of qualifying interest– (i) ten per cent of the gross amount payable in the case of housing bonds; and (ii) twenty per cent of the gross amount payable in the case of bearer instrument; and (iii) fifteen per cent of the gross amount payable in any other case; Provided that the tax paid under this paragraph is a final tax."

  — [Income Tax Act Cap. 470, as at 1 July 2026](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)
- Para 5 carries this amendment note: "(Act No. 6 of 1994, Act No. 14 of 2015, s. 18(c), Act No. 38 of 2016, s. 17(d) & (e)(ii), Act No. 15 of 2017, s. 18(c)(iv), Act No. 9 of 2018, Sch., Act No. 2 of 2020, Sch., Act No. 22 of 2020, Sch., Act No. 8 of 2021, s. 20, Act No. 4 of 2023, s. 26(b)(vii) & (viii), Act No.12 of 2024, s. 16(b)(ii), Act No. 9 of 2025, s. 30(d), Act No. 19 of 2026, s. 21(c))". The note does not say which subparagraph each Act touched. — [same](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)
- The 2017 revision has **identical** wording for 5(b)(i)-(iii) ("(iii) bearer bonds with a maturity of ten years and above, ten per cent of the gross amount payable, of the gross amount payable") and for 5(h)(i)-(iii). Its proviso reads "Provided that the tax paid under this subparagraph is final." — [Income Tax Act, Revised Edition 2018 [Rev. 2017], KRA](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/IncomeTaxAct2of1975.pdf)
- For non-residents (Head B para 3(e)), the current rates are 15% on Government bearer bonds of two years or more and other interest, 7.5% on bearer bonds issued outside Kenya of two years or more, and 25% on other bearer instruments. There is no ten-year rule. — [Income Tax Act as at 1 July 2026](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf). The 7.5% rate (down from 15%) came from the Finance Act 2022. — [EY, Kenya enacts Finance Act 2022](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2022-5626)
- PwC's tax summary lists "bearer bonds with a maturity of 10 years or more" at 10%. That matches the bearer-only wording of the statute, not a rule covering every bond. — [PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, Kenya withholding taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/kenya/corporate/withholding-taxes) (search snippet only; page not fetched; undated)
- **Market and CBK practice differs from the literal text.** The repository's legal review records that the owner-supplied December 2026 CBK prospectus for the re-opened FXD1/2020/015, a registered bond with 15-year original tenor, prints "WITHHOLDING TAX 10%". — `/home/user/Mwangaza-Yield/docs/overhaul/LEGAL-REVIEW.md` (internal, primary CBK document as transcribed there)

### Inferences
- **(a) Bearer instruments:** 25% under 5(b)(i), or 15% for a Government bearer bond of at least two years under 5(b)(ii). For a resident individual's qualifying interest the rate is **20% under 5(h)(ii)**.
- **(b) Housing bonds:** 10% final, for a resident individual's qualifying interest only, under 5(h)(i). This applies up to the KSh 300,000 aggregate cap in the definition of qualifying interest (section 4 below).
- **(c) Ten years and above:** 10% under 5(b)(iii), but the statute says "**bearer bonds**". It does not say "bonds, loans or other financial instruments". The phrase "bonds, loans or other financial instruments with a maturity of 10 years and above" in the brief does **not** appear in the Act's text. Searches found no Act using that phrasing.
- **(d) Other interest, including Government bonds under 10 years:** 15% under 5(b)(ii) "and other sources", and 15% final for resident individuals under 5(h)(iii).
- **Conflict to flag.** The 10% that CBK and the market apply to registered Treasury bonds of ten or more years has no explicit statutory hook in the 5(h) qualifying-interest rule. It rests on 5(b)(iii), whose words cover bearer bonds. CBK may read 5(b)(iii) broadly, or rely on an administrative position or exemption order that I did not find (for example, a section 35(7) Gazette order). This is unconfirmed. Applying 10% to registered bonds of ten or more years is **CBK practice**, evidenced by a primary CBK prospectus. Statute, read literally, gives 15% for a resident individual. Do not present the 10% as "the statute says".

### Gaps
- I could not find which Act first inserted 5(b)(iii) "bearer bonds with a maturity of ten years and above". It is already in the Rev. 2017 text. The consolidation's notes for para 5 name nothing between 1994 and 2015, which suggests it predates 2014. I found no Big Four analysis dating it, and searches on the 2008-2010 Finance Acts returned nothing.
- I found no KRA notice, Gazette exemption order or CBK circular that extends 10% to non-bearer, registered bonds.

## 2. When the 10% rule came in, and Finance Acts 2023-2026

### Takeaway
None of the Finance Act 2023, the Tax Laws (Amendment) Act 2024, the Finance Act 2025 or the Finance Act 2026 changed the interest subparagraphs 5(b)(i)-(iii) or 5(h). The wording is identical in the 2017 revision and the 1 July 2026 text. The Finance Act 2026's only paragraph-5 amendment adds scrap metal and winnings. The Finance Bill 2024 was withdrawn, so there is no Finance Act 2024. The 2024 changes came through the Tax Laws (Amendment) Act 2024 (Act No. 12 of 2024).

### Cited findings
- Finance Act 2026: "No. 19 of 2026, Date of Assent: 23rd June, 2026". Section 21 amends Head B of the Third Schedule: "(a) in paragraph 2, by deleting subparagraph (i); (b) in paragraph 3 … inserting … (x) in respect of the sale of scrap metal, one and a half per cent … (y) in respect of winnings, twenty per cent; (c) in paragraph 5, inserting the following new subparagraphs immediately after subparagraph (p)- (q) in respect of the sale of scrap metal … (r) in respect of winnings, twenty per cent." Nothing on interest. — [Finance Act 2026, Kenya Gazette Supplement No. 157 (KRA copy)](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Finance-Act-2026.pdf)
- Finance Act 2026 s.19 amends First Schedule Part I only at paragraph 53 (pension benefits on death) and a new paragraph 76 (capital gains on transfers to a REIT). Paragraphs 51 and 60 are not touched. — [Finance Act 2026](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Finance-Act-2026.pdf)
- Finance Act 2025 (Act No. 9 of 2025): EY reports assent on 26 June 2025; another source gives 27 June. The withholding changes reported include repealing scrap-metal withholding and exempting some national-carrier payments to non-residents. No change to interest withholding was reported. — [EY, Kenya enacts Finance Act 2025, 16 July 2025](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2025-1498)
- Finance Bill 2024 withdrawn, Tax Laws (Amendment) Act 2024 enacted: assent 11 December 2024, in force 27 December 2024. It followed the rejection of the Finance Bill 2024 and revived some of its proposals. — [EY, 14 January 2025](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2025-0233); [KPMG, Tax Laws (Amendment) Act 2024 analysis](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ke/pdf/tax/2024/Tax%20Laws%20%28Amendment%29%20Act%202024%20Analysis_final.pdf) (search snippets; PDFs not fetched)
- Finance Act 2023 (Act No. 4 of 2023): summaries found (EY, PwC, KPMG, CDH) report no change to interest withholding rates for government securities. — [EY, Kenya enacts tax changes under Finance Act 2023](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/kenya-enacts-tax-changes-under-finance-act--2023) (search-level; absence of mention, not affirmative evidence)

### Inferences
- The current 10% / 15% / 20% / 25% interest structure has **not changed since at least the 2017 revision**. Comparing the two primary texts gives high confidence in that.
- The amending Acts cited against para 5 (2020-2026) do not alter the wording of the interest subparagraphs. Comparing the texts shows their changes went into other subparagraphs (fees, rent, digital, scrap, winnings and so on).

### Gaps
- The year the 10%-at-ten-years rule was introduced is still unknown (see section 1). No source dated it.

## 3. First Schedule, Part I: the infrastructure-bond exemption

### Takeaway
Two paragraphs of **First Schedule Part I** now exempt interest from listed infrastructure-type bonds with a maturity of at least three years:
- **Para 51:** infrastructure and other social services; Act No. 10 of 2006 and Act No. 10 of 2010.
- **Para 60:** infrastructure, Green-Bond-Standards projects and assets, and other social services; introduced by the Finance Act 2019.

Both survive unamended in the 1 July 2026 text. Proposals to remove or narrow the exemption were **not enacted**:
- the Tax Laws (Amendment) Bill 2020, which proposed deletion and was rejected;
- the Finance Bill 2024 (5% withholding for newly listed bonds), which was withdrawn;
- the same 5% proposal in the Tax Laws (Amendment) Bill 2024, which was dropped from the Act.

Part II of the First Schedule, the old list of exempt securities, was repealed by Act No. 2 of 2020.

### Cited findings
- Para 51, verbatim: "51. Interest income accruing from all listed bonds, notes or other similar securities used to raise funds for infrastructure and other social services, provided that such bonds, notes or securities shall have a maturity of at least three years. [Act No. 10 of 2006, s. 29, Act No. 10 of 2010, s. 32.]" — [Income Tax Act as at 1 July 2026](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf). The same text and note appear in the Rev. 2017 edition. — [KRA Rev. 2017](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/IncomeTaxAct2of1975.pdf)
- Para 60, verbatim: "60. Interest income accruing from all listed bonds, notes or other similar securities used to raise funds for infrastructure, projects and assets defined under Green Bonds Standards and Guidelines, and other social services: Provided that such bonds, notes or securities shall have a maturity of at least three years." No amendment note is printed. — [Income Tax Act as at 1 July 2026](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)
- Para 60 came from the **Finance Act 2019** (Act No. 23 of 2019), effective 1 January 2020 per RSM: "introduced exemption on interest income accruing from all listed bonds … used to raise funds for infrastructure, projects and assets defined under Green Bond Standards and Guidelines". — [RSM Kenya, Finance Act 2019](https://www.rsm.global/kenya/insights/tax-insights/kenya-finance-act-2019); [EY, Kenya enacts Finance Act 2019, 15 Nov 2019](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2019-2045-kenya-enacts-finance-act-2019)
- The Tax Laws (Amendment) Bill 2020 proposed removing the exemption. Parliament rejected that: Grant Thornton records "Exemption nullified — Proposal rejected", and Bowmans calls it "reinstating the exemption". — [Grant Thornton, Tax Laws (Amendment) Act 2020](https://www.grantthornton.co.ke/globalassets/1.-member-firms/kenya/insights/pdf/grant-thornton-alert---the-tax-laws-amendment-act-2020---final.pdf); [Bowmans](https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/overview-an-analysis-of-the-tax-laws-amendment-act-2020/) (search snippets)
- The Finance Bill 2024 proposed to "repeal an income tax exemption on interest income earned from all listed bonds … infrastructure, projects and assets defined under Green Bonds Standards and Guidelines, and other social services that have a maturity of at least three years. Withholding tax at the rate of 5% will apply", with bonds listed before commencement grandfathered. The Bill was withdrawn after public rejection. — [EY, Kenya proposes tax changes under the Finance Bill 2024](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/kenya-proposes-tax-changes-under-the-finance-bill--2024); [EY GTNU 2024-1032](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2024-1032)
- The Tax Laws (Amendment) Bill 2024 repeated the 5% proposal. Per Bowmans, the enacted Act **kept** the exemption and dropped the 5%. — [KPMG, TLA and TPA Bills 2024 analysis](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ke/pdf/tax/2024/Tax%20Laws%20(Amendment)%20and%20Tax%20Procedures%20(Ammendment)%20Bills,%202024%20Analysis.pdf); [Bowmans](https://bowmanslaw.com/?p=70054) (search snippet; page returned 403 on fetch). The primary 2026 text corroborates this independently: para 60 carries no 2024 amendment, and Act No. 12 of 2024 touched only para 53 and the second para 57 in Part I.
- Section 2 defines "infrastructure bond" as "a bond issued by the Government for the financing of a strategic public infrastructure facility including a road, hospital, port, sporting facility, water and sewerage system, a communication network or energy project". The Finance Bill 2021 proposed adding this definition because the law then had none. — [Income Tax Act as at 1 July 2026](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf); [PwC, Finance Bill 2021 highlights](https://www.pwc.com/ke/en/assets/pdf/tax-alert-highlights-of-the-finance-bill-2021.pdf) (search snippet)
- First Schedule Part II, "SECURITIES, THE INTEREST ON WHICH IS EXEMPT FROM TAX — Repealed by Act No. 2 of 2020, Sch." In Rev. 2017 it listed only colonial-era stocks (for example "Kenya Government 2¾ per cent Stock 1977/83"), and only for interest paid to non-residents. — [2026 text](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf); [Rev. 2017](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/IncomeTaxAct2of1975.pdf)
- Section 35(4): "No deduction shall be made under subsection (1) or (3) from a payment which is income exempt from tax under this Act". So no withholding applies to exempt IFB interest. — [2026 text](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- The exemption **is** limited to (i) **listed** securities, (ii) proceeds used for infrastructure, social services or green assets, and (iii) a **maturity of at least three years**. Kenyan IFBs are listed on the NSE and have far longer than three years, so they qualify. The paragraphs do not use the defined term "infrastructure bond", so eligibility turns on listing and use of funds, not on the CBK label alone.
- Enacted and proposed must be kept separate. **Enacted:** introduction (2006 and 2010 for para 51; 2019 for para 60). **Proposed and not enacted:** removal (TLA Bill 2020), 5% withholding (Finance Bill 2024 and TLA Bill 2024). I found no enacted narrowing.

### Gaps
- Whether a Finance Bill 2023 or 2025 proposal touched the IFB exemption. I found none in the summaries searched (absence of evidence only).
- Why the 2026 consolidation prints no amendment note for para 60 (presumably Act No. 23 of 2019).

## 4. Is withholding on interest a final tax for resident individuals?

### Takeaway
**Yes.** A resident individual's interest, discount or original issue discount is "qualifying interest". It is taxed at the qualifying-interest rates in Head B para 5(h), and "the tax paid under this paragraph is a final tax". Section 76A bars the Commissioner from assessing income that bore a final withholding tax. The only cap in the definition applies to **housing bonds: aggregate interest not exceeding KSh 300,000**. Ordinary qualifying interest has no cap.

### Cited findings
- Section 2, verbatim:
  > "'qualifying interest' means the aggregate interest, discount or original issue discount receivable by a resident individual in any year of income: Provided that– (a) interest earned on an account held jointly by a husband and wife shall be deemed to be qualifying interest; and (b) in the case of housing bonds, the aggregate amount of interest shall not exceed three hundred thousand shillings;"

  It also defines "'qualifying interest rate of tax' means the resident withholding tax rate in respect of interest specified in paragraph 5 of the Third Schedule". — [Income Tax Act as at 1 July 2026](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)
- Head B para 5(h) proviso: "Provided that the tax paid under this paragraph is a final tax." — [2026 text](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)
- Section 76A: "The Commissioner shall not assess any person for any year of income on that portion of income which has been subject to withholding tax which is also a final tax. [Act No. 8 of 1991, s. 67.]" — [2026 text](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)
- Section 34(1) now reads only "The tax chargeable on any income specified in this Act shall be at the rate specified in the Third Schedule." Its amendment note runs to Act No. 12 of 2024. — [2026 text](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf). In **Rev. 2017**, s.34(1)(a) excluded qualifying interest from the individual rates, and s.34(1)(c) charged it "at the qualifying interest rate of tax". — [Rev. 2017](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/IncomeTaxAct2of1975.pdf). Head B para 1 still says the individual rates apply "other than that of the total income comprising fringe benefits and the qualifying interest". — [2026 text](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)
- Section 35(3)(b) requires a payer to deduct tax on interest paid to a resident, "including interest arising from a discount upon final satisfaction or redemption of a debt, bond … measured as the original issue discount". It excludes interest paid to Fourth Schedule financial institutions or to First Schedule exempt persons. — [2026 text](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)
- The interest-deduction provision in section 15 (Part IV) defines "investment income" as dividends and interest "but excludes qualifying dividends and qualifying interest". — [2026 text](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- The finality now rests on the 5(h) proviso, reinforced by s.76A. It no longer rests on s.34(1)(c), whose text was replaced at some point between 2018 and 2024; the most likely date is Act No. 2 of 2020 or Act No. 8 of 2020, but this is unconfirmed. The final-tax treatment applies only to *resident individuals*. For companies, 5(b) withholding is not final under these words.
- The repository's note that the Tax Laws (Amendment) Act 2020 "made the 15% on qualifying interest final" is not borne out by the texts. The word "final" is already in the Rev. 2017 proviso ("Provided that the tax paid under this subparagraph is final").

### Gaps
- The exact Act that rewrote s.34(1) (the note lists Acts up to No. 12 of 2024 without subsection detail).

## 5. Is Treasury-bill interest exempt in the First Schedule?

### Takeaway
**No.** I found no First Schedule exemption for Treasury-bill interest or discount, in Part I or the repealed Part II. The statute brings **discount** into "qualifying interest" by name, so a resident individual's T-bill discount falls under para 5(h)(iii) at **15% final**. (The bearer subparagraph does not apply, because T-bills are held in CSD or DhowCSD book-entry form; that last point is an inference.)

### Cited findings
- The qualifying-interest definition covers "the aggregate interest, discount or original issue discount receivable by a resident individual". — [2026 text](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)
- A search of the whole 2026 text for "treasury bill" returns only three hits, none an exemption: (1) the s.2 definition of "qualifying assets" for an individual retirement fund ("time deposits, treasury bills, treasury bonds, securities traded…"); (2) and (3) two "market lending/interest rate" definitions keyed to "the average 91-day treasury bill rate". — [2026 text](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)
- The Part I interest exemptions present are:
  - para 49 (US Exim Bank arrangements);
  - paras 51 and 60 (listed infrastructure, social and green bonds of three years or more);
  - para 54 ("Interest income on bonds issued by the East African Development Bank", Act No. 38 of 2016);
  - para 35 ("Interest on a savings account held with the Kenya Post Office Savings Bank").

  Para 52 (asset-backed securities) was deleted by Act No. 2 of 2020. None covers T-bills. — [2026 text](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- The statute supports **15% final** on a resident individual's T-bill discount. Nothing in it supports "T-bills are tax-exempt for individuals". Market claims are another researcher's scope.

### Gaps
- Section 13(2) Gazette exemption notices and the s.35(7) power ("The Cabinet Secretary may, by notice in the Gazette, exempt from the provisions of subsection (3) … any payment or class of payments") could in principle exempt T-bill or long-bond interest outside the Act's own text. I did not search Gazette notices exhaustively. A Kenya Law exemption-notice index exists (https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/1974/93), but I did not read it.
