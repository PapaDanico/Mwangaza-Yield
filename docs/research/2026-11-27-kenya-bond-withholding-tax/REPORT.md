# CBK withholds 10% where statute says 15%

For a resident individual in November 2026, the Central Bank of Kenya withholds four rates on government-securities interest. **Treasury bonds of under ten years' original tenor pay 15%.** **Treasury bonds of ten years or more original tenor pay 10%.** **Listed infrastructure bonds pay 0%.** **Treasury bills pay 15% on the discount.** For a resident individual the deduction is a **final tax**. CBK applies it by **original tenor, not remaining term**: it printed 10% on a ten-year bond re-opened with only 3.0 years left in its 11 November 2026 switch prospectus. None of these rates changed under the Finance Acts 2023, 2025 or 2026, or under the Tax Laws (Amendment) Act 2024. The infrastructure-bond exemption survived three attempts to repeal or cut it to 5%. The finding that matters for anyone coding this is that **the 10% long-bond rate is CBK practice, not a statutory rule that clearly reaches a resident individual's registered bond**. In the Income Tax Act, 10% at ten years appears only in a subparagraph worded for "bearer bonds". The paragraph that actually governs resident individuals gives 10% only to housing bonds and 15% to everything else. The engine should still code 10%, because CBK as withholding agent deducts 10% and the investor's cash flow is what CBK pays. But the source should be labelled "CBK prospectus practice", not "the Act". The claim that T-bills are tax-exempt for individuals has no support in statute, in KRA material or in CBK documents. CBK's own November 2026 prospectus prints 15% against three T-bills.

## The Act sets 15% final for individuals and words its 10% for bearer bonds

The resident withholding rates sit in **Third Schedule, Head B, paragraph 5** of the Income Tax Act (Cap. 470). The consolidation read here is "Legislation as at 1 July 2026", published by KRA. Paragraph 5(b) covers interest generally: 25% on bearer instruments other than a Government bearer bond of at least two years, 15% on "Government Bearer Bond of at least two years duration and other sources", and 10% on "**bearer bonds with a maturity of ten years and above**" ([Income Tax Act as at 1 July 2026](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)). Paragraph 5(h) is the provision written for individuals. It taxes "qualifying interest" at "**ten per cent … in the case of housing bonds**; … twenty per cent … in the case of bearer instrument; and … **fifteen per cent … in any other case**; Provided that the tax paid under this paragraph is a final tax" ([same](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)). Section 2 defines qualifying interest as "the aggregate interest, **discount or original issue discount** receivable by a resident individual in any year of income". The definition caps housing-bond interest at **KSh 300,000** a year. Section 76A bars the Commissioner from assessing income that has borne a final withholding tax ([same](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)).

Read literally, these provisions give a resident individual **15% on a registered Treasury bond of any tenor**. Paragraph 5(h) has no ten-year rule, and the only 10% outside housing bonds is the bearer-worded 5(b)(iii). Every widely repeated summary of the rule ("10% on bonds of ten years or more") traces to that bearer subparagraph. PwC's tax summary, for example, lists the 10% as "Bearer bonds (maturity ≥ 10 years)" ([PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/kenya/corporate/withholding-taxes)). The phrase "bonds, loans or other financial instruments with a maturity of ten years and above" does not appear in the Act. No KRA notice, Gazette exemption order under section 35(7), or CBK circular was found that extends 10% to registered bonds. One hypothesis is that CBK reads "bearer bonds" broadly, or as a legacy of the era when Kenyan government paper was issued in bearer form. That hypothesis is untested.

This structure is old. Paragraphs 5(b)(i)-(iii) and 5(h) are worded identically in KRA's Revised Edition 2018 [Rev. 2017] ([KRA Rev. 2017](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/IncomeTaxAct2of1975.pdf)). That corrects a line in the repository's CLAUDE.md, which says the Rev. 2017 text "does not contain the 10%-at-ten-years" rule. It does contain the rule, but only for bearer bonds. Finality is not new either. The Rev. 2017 proviso already reads "the tax paid under this subparagraph is final". What the Tax Laws (Amendment) Act 2020 changed was the *scope* of qualifying interest. Before 2020, only interest from banks, financial institutions, building societies, **the Central Bank of Kenya** and CS-approved bonds qualified. From 2020, any resident individual's interest subject to 15% withholding qualifies ([RSM Kenya on TLAA 2020](https://www.rsm.global/kenya/sites/default/files/media/rsm_kenya_income_tax_amendments_-_tax_laws_amendment_act_2020.pdf)). Government-securities interest paid through CBK was therefore final before and after 2020. Finality covers resident individuals only. For resident companies, trusts and clubs, the same deduction is an advance against tax, not a final tax ([RSM Kenya Tax Guide 2025/26](https://www.rsm.global/kenya/sites/default/files/media/EA%20Booklets/RSMEA%20Kenya%20Tax%20Guide%202025-26%20-%202026.pdf)).

No recent Act touched any of this. The Finance Act 2026 (Act No. 19, assent 23 June 2026) amends paragraph 5 only by adding scrap metal and winnings ([Finance Act 2026](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Finance-Act-2026.pdf)). Commentary on the Finance Act 2025 and the Finance Act 2023 reports no change to interest withholding ([EY on Finance Act 2025](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2025-1498); [EY on Finance Act 2023](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/kenya-enacts-tax-changes-under-finance-act--2023)). The Income Tax (Withholding Tax) Rules 2026, in force 28 August 2026, are procedural and "do not … provide new withholding tax rates" ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/10/kenya-new-withholding-tax-rules.html)).

## CBK prints 10% by original tenor, down to three years remaining

CBK is the issuer and the paying agent, and its prospectuses are the operative documents for what an investor actually receives. They are consistent across 2023-2026. Every bond of **ten years' original tenor or longer is printed at 10%**, however little time it has left. Every 2-, 3- and 5-year bond is printed at 15%. The July 2023 prospectus taxed the re-opened FXD1/2016/10, "3.2 years to maturity", at 10% alongside 15% for a new five-year bond ([CBK, July 2023](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/511717136_JULY%202023%20FXD1-2023-005%20AND%20FXD1-2016-010%20DATED%2017.07.23.pdf)). June 2024 printed 15% for FXD1/2023/02, /2024/03 and /2023/05, and 10% for FXD1/2023/10 ([CBK, June 2024](https://centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/1509919353_JUNE%202024%20FXD1-2023-002%20AND%20FXD1-2024-003%20DATED%2010.06.2024%20%20AND%20FXD1-2023-005%20AND%20FXD1-2023-10%20DATED%2017.06.2024.pdf)). January 2025 printed 10% for a 15-year bond with 8.3 years left ([CBK, Jan 2025](https://centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/251215993_JANUARY%202025%20FXD1-2018-015%20AND%20FXD1-2022-025%20DATED%2020.01.2025.pdf)). The current table-form prospectuses show the same split. The 23 November 2026 re-opening prints "WITHHOLDING TAX 10% 10%" for FXD1/2019/015, labelled "Fifteen (7.3 years to maturity)", and for SDB1/2011/030, a 30-year bond with 14.3 years left ([CBK, Nov 2026](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/633889179_NOVEMBER%202026%20FXD1-2019-015%20AND%20SDB1-2011-030%20DATED%20%2023-11-2026.pdf)).

The clearest single document is the **11 November 2026 switch prospectus**. Its row reads "15%" for the three T-bills, "15%" for the five-year FXD1/2023/005 with 1.7 years left, and "**10%**" for FXD4/2019/010, labelled "**Ten Years (3.0 yrs to maturity)**" ([CBK switch, Nov 2026](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/1533456351_NOVEMBER%202026%20SWITCH%20FROM%20FXD1-2023-005,%202696-091,%202657-182,%202585-364%20TO%20FXD4-2019-010%20DATED%2011-11-2026%20REVISED.pdf)). A remaining-term rule would have put that bond at 15%. CBK's older investor FAQ states the rule in words: "Withholding Tax of 15% on Treasury bonds with tenor up to 9 years and 10% for Treasury bonds of tenor of 10 years or more is charged on the discount as well as periodic interest payment". It adds that "withholding tax is computed on the Clean Price" and that settlement is "the Dirty Price plus Withholding Tax" ([CBK FAQ on GoK Securities](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/images/FAQs/Frequently%20Asked%20Questions%20on%20GoK%20Securities.pdf)). Two operational facts follow. The tax falls on the purchase discount as well as on coupons. And it is collected at settlement on the clean price, not on accrued interest.

Nothing in writing says "original tenor". That reading rests on consistent practice and on CBK's own labelling, which puts the issued tenor first ("Ten Years (3.0 yrs…)"). The nine-to-ten-year boundary is untested, because no FXD of six to nine years' original tenor was issued in 2023-2026. One press source conflicts. A Nation article puts the 10% threshold at "more than five years" ([Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/business/inside-kenya-s-costly-sh1-6trn-infrastructure-bonds-binge--4533170)). The statute, the FAQ and every prospectus contradict it, so it should be disregarded.

## Infrastructure bonds stay exempt after three failed repeal attempts

The infrastructure exemption has a clear statutory basis, unlike the long-bond rate. **First Schedule Part I, paragraph 51**, from Acts 10 of 2006 and 10 of 2010, exempts "Interest income accruing from all listed bonds, notes or other similar securities used to raise funds for infrastructure and other social services, provided that such bonds … shall have a maturity of at least three years". **Paragraph 60**, from the Finance Act 2019, extends this to green-bond assets. Both are unamended in the 1 July 2026 text ([Income Tax Act as at 1 July 2026](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf); [RSM on Finance Act 2019](https://www.rsm.global/kenya/insights/tax-insights/kenya-finance-act-2019)). Section 35(4) forbids withholding from exempt income. Eligibility turns on three conditions: the bond is listed, its proceeds fund infrastructure, and its maturity is at least three years. The defined term "infrastructure bond" does not appear in either paragraph.

**No proposal to tax new infrastructure bonds has been enacted.** The Tax Laws (Amendment) Bill 2020 proposed deleting the exemption and Parliament rejected it ([Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.co.ke/globalassets/1.-member-firms/kenya/insights/pdf/grant-thornton-alert---the-tax-laws-amendment-act-2020---final.pdf)). The Finance Bill 2024 proposed repealing it for newly listed bonds and applying 5% withholding, with existing bonds grandfathered. That Bill was withdrawn ([EY on Finance Bill 2024](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/kenya-proposes-tax-changes-under-the-finance-bill--2024)). The Tax Laws (Amendment) Bill 2024 repeated the 5% proposal, and the enacted Act dropped it ([KPMG analysis](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ke/pdf/tax/2024/Tax%20Laws%20(Amendment)%20and%20Tax%20Procedures%20(Ammendment)%20Bills,%202024%20Analysis.pdf)). The 2026 text confirms this independently: paragraph 60 carries no 2024 amendment. CBK practice matches the statute. The 2024 prospectuses say "The Bond will be tax free as is the case for Infrastructure Bonds as provided for under the income Tax Act" ([CBK, Feb 2024](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/2087959385_February%202024%20IFB1-2024-8.5%20%20DATED%2019-02-2024.pdf)). The August 2026 re-opening of IFB1/2019/016, IFB1/2021/018 and IFB1/2021/021 prints "WITHHOLDING TAX (%) N/A" and "The bond does not attract withholding tax" ([CBK, Aug 2026](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/618766331_AUGUST%202026%20IFB1-2019-016,%20IFB1-2021-018%20AND%20IFB1-2021-021%20DATED%20%2017-08-2026--.pdf)). One edge case is open. It is unclear whether an IFB with less than three years *remaining* still meets the "maturity of at least three years" test. No prospectus has tested it, and CBK printed N/A on an IFB with 5.8 years left.

## Treasury bills pay 15%; the "exempt" claim traces to one blog

No First Schedule paragraph exempts Treasury-bill discount. A search of the 2026 text for "treasury bill" returns only a retirement-fund asset definition and two references to the 91-day rate ([Income Tax Act as at 1 July 2026](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)). Qualifying interest expressly includes "discount", and a book-entry bill in DhowCSD is neither a housing bond nor a bearer instrument. A resident individual's bill discount therefore falls under 5(h)(iii) at **15% final**. CBK collects the tax up front. In the FAQ's worked example, a 91-day bill on KSh 12m priced at KSh 11,775,360 yields a gross discount of KSh 224,640 and a net of **KSh 190,944** after 15%. The arithmetic reconciles exactly (224,640 × 0.85). The exemption applies only "on presentation of Tax Exemption Certificate from the Kenya Revenue Authority" ([CBK FAQ](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/images/FAQs/Frequently%20Asked%20Questions%20on%20GoK%20Securities.pdf)). Independent tables agree. Clearstream (29 May 2026) lists "Treasury bills: 15%" ([Clearstream](https://clearstream.com/clearstream-en/res-library/market-coverage/market-taxation-guide-kenya-5116362)), and PwC, reviewed after the Finance Act 2026, keeps "Qualifying interest: Other 15" ([PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/kenya/corporate/withholding-taxes)). CBK's 11 November 2026 switch prospectus prints 15% against bills 2696/091, 2657/182 and 2585/364 ([CBK switch, Nov 2026](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/1533456351_NOVEMBER%202026%20SWITCH%20FROM%20FXD1-2023-005,%202696-091,%202657-182,%202585-364%20TO%20FXD4-2019-010%20DATED%2011-11-2026%20REVISED.pdf)).

The opposing claim comes from Serrari Markets: "Interest on Kenyan Treasury bills is exempt from withholding tax for individual investors". It cites no law ([Serrari](https://serrarigroup.com/?p=39581)). Serrari's own beginner's guide contradicts it by adding tax at settlement ([Serrari guide](https://serrarigroup.com/serrari-treasury-bills/)). The likely sources of the confusion are real exemptions that do not apply here. One is the FAQ's "tax-exempt investor" column, which needs a KRA certificate. Another is the IFB exemption. A third is PwC's treaty note that "Interest paid by the government and the Central Bank of Kenya is tax-exempt", which applies only to treaty non-residents from Denmark, Germany, Norway and the UK. Exempt institutions are a fourth: registered pension schemes, NSSF and similar bodies hold certificates ([RSM on TLAA 2020](https://www.rsm.global/kenya/sites/default/files/media/rsm_kenya_income_tax_amendments_-_tax_laws_amendment_act_2020.pdf)).

## Housing and bearer rates apply in law but not to CBK paper

The two remaining categories are real in law, but no CBK-issued security in these notes uses them. **Housing bonds** pay **10% final** under 5(h)(i), and only up to **KSh 300,000** of aggregate interest a year. The treatment of interest above that cap is not spelled out. The most natural reading is that the excess is no longer qualifying interest. **Bearer instruments** pay **20% final** for a resident individual under 5(h)(ii) ([Income Tax Act as at 1 July 2026](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf)). Two parts of the statute overlap here. Subparagraph 5(b) gives a bearer bond of ten years or more 10% and a Government bearer bond of two years or more 15%, while 5(h)(ii) gives any bearer instrument held by an individual 20%. The specific qualifying-interest provision, 5(h), should prevail for individuals, which is why the decision table uses 20%. Treasury bonds and bills are book-entry securities in DhowCSD, so neither category should normally fire in the engine. Savings Development Bonds (SDB1/2011/030) are taxed as ordinary long bonds at 10%.

## Decision table for `determineWHTRate`

The repository's current function, `if (taxExempt) return 0; return tenorYears >= 10 ? 0.1 : 0.15` (`src/lib/financial-engine.ts:68`), matches CBK practice, provided `tenorYears` is the **original** tenor from the issue code's last segment (FXD4/2019/**010**). One caller does not meet that condition. `realCurveRows` (`src/lib/real-curve.ts:138`) passes `cell.years`, which is the bucket of *years remaining at auction* (`src/lib/yield-curve.ts:131`). A ten-year bond re-opened with three years left therefore lands in the 5y bucket and is netted at 15%, where CBK withheld 10%. Within the 7-12 year bucket, the same code nets a 15-year bond with 7.3 years left correctly only because of the bucket's label. A per-bond WHT applied before bucketing would fix it.

## Conclusion

The common shorthand "15% under ten years, 10% at ten or more" is right about the money and wrong about its source. The 10% rate rests on how CBK, as withholding agent, administers a bearer-worded subparagraph. For a resident individual's registered bond, the Act read literally points to 15%. That has two consequences. A future KRA position or a Finance Act clean-up could move long bonds to 15% without anyone "changing the rate". And any page that tells readers "the Income Tax Act gives 10%" overstates the law. A source note reading "CBK prospectus, original tenor" is defensible. The original-tenor rule also cuts against intuition in a way that already shows up in the codebase. A curve built on years remaining will tax a ten-year bond with three years left at 15%, when CBK took 10%.

Two documents would settle what remains, and both are reachable through the `cbk-gaps` route rather than by search. A KRA practice note or section 35(7) Gazette order on long-bond interest would confirm or remove the 10% statutory gap. The DhowCSD FAQ (December 2025) would show whether CBK now states "original tenor" in words. Until then the decision table above is safe to code. Its only medium-or-lower rows are the statutory basis of the 10%, the housing-bond cap, and the bearer overlap, and none of these change what a DhowCSD investor is actually paid.
