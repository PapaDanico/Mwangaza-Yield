- **ODPC Guidance Note on Registration of Data Controllers and Processors.** Registration fees run from Kshs 4,000 to Kshs 40,000. A private-sector entity that processes personal data of people in Kenya must register if it has annual turnover or revenue of Kshs 5 million or more, or more than 10 employees, **unless it is a "non-exempt mandatory registration entity", which must register whatever its size**. The sectors listed for that category include **financial services** and **education**, beside political canvassing, crime prevention, gambling, health administration, hospitality, property management, telecommunications, direct marketing, transport and genetic data. Such an entity with turnover under Kshs 5 million and fewer than ten employees pays Kshs 4,000. Fee bands for small, medium and large controllers follow (medium: 51 to 99 employees and turnover up to Kshs 50 million, Kshs 16,000, or Kshs 9,000 on the second column; large: more than 99 employees). **This settles the opening of the exemption test that was cut off before, and it changes the earlier note: the small-entity exemption does not by itself answer the question for a financial-services or education business.** What the text does not settle is whether a site that stores nothing on a server and only reads figures typed into the reader's own browser "processes personal data" at all; that, not the size, is the live question. ## 18 November 2026: primary texts read, and the notices refined

`odpc.go.ke` and `cma.or.ke` were reachable from the session (HTTP 200), so these were read from the instruments themselves, not from summaries.

- **Data Protection (General) Regulations, 2021 (ODPC copy).** Reg. 23 requires a controller to "develop, publish and regularly update a policy reflecting their personal data handling practices", and lists what it may cover: the nature of data held, how to access it and exercise rights, a complaints mechanism, lawful purpose, transfers outside Kenya and where possible the recipients, the retention period and schedule (reg. 19), and the collection of children's data. Response times: an access request within **seven days**, a rectification request within **fourteen days**, an erasure request within **fourteen days**.
- **ODPC Guidance Note on Data Protection Policy (April 2026).** A privacy notice should tell people their rights, the purpose of collection, third-party recipients and safeguards, the security measures, and the consequences of not providing data; be available before or as soon as possible after collection; and be in plain language, ideally in short sections. It says a cookie banner is "imperative" for a site that uses cookies. Neither site sets cookies.
- **Capital Markets (Licensing Requirements) (General) Regulations, 2025 (LN 197 of 2025, CMA copy).** Reg. 12(1): no person may "engage in the business of an investment adviser or holdout as an investment adviser" without a licence. The definition of "investment advisor" includes "digital platforms that provide automated, algorithm-driven investment advisory services with little to no human supervision". Reg. 14 adds conditions for such platforms (principal bank account in Kenya, capital, systems, documented methodologies, risk framework, client on-boarding, a data protection policy and seven years' record keeping). Licence fees are in the Sixth Schedule (investment adviser: 10,000 application, 100,000 and 100,000). **Nothing in the text says whether a free, non-transacting calculator "provides investment advisory services"; that is the question for counsel.** The "holdout" wording is why neither site should describe its output as advice or recommendation. The earlier open row on this instrument is closed as to the text; the legal question is not.
- **Changes made.** Mwangaza's privacy notice gains a plain statement on cookies and local storage, a short "why and for how long" list, the seven and fourteen day response times, and drops a sentence that told readers there was "no question" whether the operator is a data controller (that is a legal conclusion, and the ODPC registration note lists financial services and education among the sectors that register whatever their size). The FAQ no longer says "no data collection" (the first-party tool counter exists) and links to the funding page. The footer says the site is not a licensed investment adviser, matching the terms and disclaimer.
- **Not changed, deliberately.** No page claims ODPC registration or exemption, and none should until counsel has answered whether a site that stores nothing on a server processes personal data at all.

## 21 November 2026: research pass on the two open legal-tax questions

**Withholding on bonds (10% at ten years, 0% infrastructure).** Searched again; no primary text surfaced from a session. Secondary sources agree on the shape: a KPMG 2020 fiscal guide lists 15% on government securities, 10% on bonds of ten years or more, infrastructure-bond interest exempt; 2026 investor guides repeat it. Two facts cut the other way and are why nothing changes in the engine: the Tax Laws (Amendment) Act 2020 removed Part II of the First Schedule (the old exemption list) and made the 15% on qualifying interest final, and the Finance Bill 2024 proposed repealing the exemption for listed infrastructure and social bonds of three years or more (enactment not confirmed). Treat 10% at ten years as **market practice, probably correct, unconfirmed**, and the 0% for infrastructure bonds as **the one most likely to be out of date**. To settle both from the statute, `cbk-gaps` now also reads KRA's consolidated *Income Tax Act 2026* (extra doc, rev 1, windows around "ten years", "infrastructure bond", "treasury bill", "qualifying interest", "bearer"); the next hourly run that picks it up posts it to the `gapdocs` field. Apply the outcome in `determineWHTRate` and JiPange's `rates-feed.ts` only once the text is read.

**LN 197 (investment adviser, "holdout").** No CMA guidance or commentary found on whether a free, non-transacting calculator is an "investment adviser". Recommended posture, mine and not counsel's: stay on the calculation side of the line. Every output is a computation from stated inputs and a dated, sourced figure; nothing tells a reader what to buy, bid or sell; no personal risk profiling. On 21 Nov two advice-like phrases were reworded ("Back to our picks" is now "Back to the default selection"; "recommended for retail" is now "the option most retail bidders use"). The auction bid guidance is the closest feature to advice; it is presented as a measured historical range with its published accuracy, which is the defensible form, and should stay worded that way.
