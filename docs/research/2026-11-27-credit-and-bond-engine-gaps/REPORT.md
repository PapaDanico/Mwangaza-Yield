# Fix five conventions, one amortisation rule

The two engines get different kinds of thing wrong. **The bond engine's arithmetic is right, but its amortisation model and data are not.** CBK's pricing rests on three rules: discount at half the quoted yield per 182-day period, accrue at coupon × days / 364, and settle at the dirty price. Run against CBK's printed tables, those rules reproduced **every row tested to 4 decimals, across 7 bonds in 3 prospectuses (2024–2026)**. Mwangaza already uses them. What fails is amortisation. CBK states each redemption step as a **fraction of the outstanding balance**, and quotes prices **per 100 of currently outstanding face**. `src/lib/sell.ts` reads fractions as shares of *original* face. `public/data/bonds.json` holds no amortisation schedule at all, and the purchase-yield engine assumes a bullet repayment, which misprices CBK's own table rows by up to **3.2 points per 100**. **The loan comparison's problem is the opposite: its rates are roughly right, but its conventions are not.** JiPange's `lib/loan-comparison.ts` uses about the right headline rates: 1% a month reducing for a SACCO, ~15% for a bank, 7.5% for M-Shwari, and Safaricom's real Fuliza bands. But its single "APR" column mixes **five different annualisation conventions**. It leaves out M-Shwari's excise. And it stretches a 61-day digital loan and a 30-day M-Shwari loan across multi-year terms that neither product offers. Kenyan rules set the shilling **Total Cost of Credit (TCC)** as the disclosure for both banks and digital lenders, with an APR as its numerical form. No rule says how to compare a 30-day fee product with a five-year loan, so the calculator has to pick a method and say which one it picked. Tax treatment is covered in the earlier report, `reports/Kenya bond withholding tax rules.md`, and is not repeated here.

## Kenyan loan prices are quoted in four incompatible units

Each product family prices in its own unit, and a comparison only works once they are on one footing. **SACCOs** quote per month. The usual figure is **1% a month on a reducing balance (12% nominal, 12.68% effective)** ([businessradar](https://businessradar.co.ke/sacco-loan-comparison-kenya/)), with limits of **3–4× deposits** ([paybillke](https://paybillke.com/calculators/sacco-loan-calculator)). Neither figure is regulated. The SASRA framework (the Sacco Societies Act and LN 95/2010 and LN 82/2020) turned up no cap on rates or fees ([Kenya Law LN 95/2010](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/2010/95/eng@2022-12-31)). Guides also disagree on the repayment method. One says "almost all" SACCOs quote a **flat** rate ([paybillke](https://paybillke.com/guides/sacco-loan-vs-bank-loan-vs-mobile-app-kenya)), and a flat 1% a month works out to about **22–24% on a reducing basis**. So the method has to be a user input, not a constant. SACCOs also sit outside the regulated TCC website, and a CBK survey headline says they have "the most hidden loan charges" ([The Standard](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/business/article/2001432066/saccos-have-the-most-hidden-loan-charges-cbk-survey-shows)). For a SACCO loan, the calculator must build the APR itself from the rate, the method and the fees.

**Banks** quote a nominal annual rate on a reducing balance. Under CBK's revised risk-based pricing model, that rate is a reference rate (CBR or KESONIA) plus a borrower-specific premium K, plus fees ([CBK RBCPM](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Revised-Risk-based-Credit-Pricing-Model.pdf)). **CBR and KESONIA converged at 8.75%**. **27 of 37 banks chose CBR as their reference rate**, and the **mean bank lending rate was 14.5% in May 2026**, down from a 15.4% peak ([Business Daily, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/health/uniform-cost-of-credit-as-base-loan-rates-converge-at-8-75pc-5540714)). The MPC held CBR at 8.75% on 7 October 2026 ([CBK](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/2026/10/07/mpc-retains-the-cbr-at-8-75-percent-3/)). JiPange's 15% is therefore a fair placeholder. It is not an APR, though, because fees are what separate the nominal rate from the cost. One press example shows a KSh 1m, 60-month unsecured loan at an **APR of 16.63%** ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/markets/capital-markets/kenya-s-most-expensive-cheapest-loans-revealed-3323990)).

**CBK-licensed digital lenders** quote per day or per four weeks. Tala's own FAQ gives interest "as low as 0.3% per day", charged only for the days used. Repayment can fall on any day from **1 to 61**, and a **one-off 8% charge** applies to any balance unpaid on day 61. Since **27 December 2024**, the 20% excise no longer applies to Tala's loan interest ([Tala FAQ](https://tala.co.ke/loc-faq-1/)). Tala's printed APR range of **109.5–219%** is simply 0.3–0.6% × 365, with no compounding ([Tala](https://tala.co.ke/?p=8651)). Branch is reported at **8.7–19.5% per four weeks** ([paybillke](https://paybillke.com/guides/kenya-loan-apps-effective-apr-2026)), but no Branch primary page was read. Zenka's 36%-a-month terms were rejected by a court as punitive ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/court-rejects-punitive-438pc-interest-on-digital-loan-5532234)). CBK had licensed **281 digital credit providers** by 30 September 2026 ([CBK DCP directory](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Directory-of-Digital-Credit-Providers-September-2026.pdf)).

**M-Shwari and Fuliza** charge fees, not interest. **M-Shwari** charges a **7.5% facility fee per 30-day loan**, plus excise of 20% of the fee (**1.5% of principal**), which is **deducted at disbursement** ([NCBA Key Facts, June 2023](https://ke.ncbagroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/M-Shwari-Key-Facts-21-June-23.pdf); [NCBA FAQ, Feb 2024](https://ke.ncbagroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/CHANGE-OF-EXCISE-DUTY-COLLECTION-PROCESS-FAQs.pdf); [The Star](https://the-star.co.ke/news/realtime/2024-03-08-kra-to-deduct-excise-duty-on-m-shwari-loans-at-disbursement-from-friday)). So a KSh 1,000 loan **pays out KSh 985 and is repaid with KSh 1,075** ([businessradar](https://businessradar.co.ke/?p=6652)). That is a cost of 90 on 985 received: **9.14% per 30 days, about 111% nominal and about 190% compounded**. Because the fee is flat, repaying on day 1 costs the same as repaying on day 30. **Fuliza** charges a 1% access fee plus a fixed shilling fee per day, set by the outstanding balance:

| Balance (KSh) | Daily fee before excise (KSh) |
|---|---|
| 1–100 | 0 |
| 101–500 | 2.50 |
| 501–1,000 | 5 |
| 1,001–1,500 | 18 |
| 1,501–2,500 | 20 |
| 2,501–70,000 | 25 |

The limit is **KSh 70,000** ([PesaTrail, verified 11 Aug 2026](https://pesatrail.com/fuliza-guide-2026.html)). Balances of KSh 1,000 or less get a **3-day waiver** ([Business Today](https://businesstoday.co.ke/safaricom-slashes-fuliza-charges-see-new-tariff-and-more-details/)). Excise of 20% brings the top band to **KSh 30 a day** ([Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/business/m-pesa-fuliza-charges-across-all-loan-bands/), search summary). A claimed "June 2026 revision" appears only on a blog ([Shmoney](https://shmoney.co.ke/blog/fuliza-charges-2026-daily-fees-real-cost-how-to-track-them/)), and it is almost certainly the October 2022 cut given the wrong date. JiPange's bands match the tariff.

## Kenyan rules prescribe a shilling TCC, and leave the annualisation to the calculator

For banks and microfinance banks, the regulated comparison is the CBK/KBA site costofcredit.co.ke. It defines **"total cost of credit = KESONIA/CBR + Premium (K) + Fees and Charges"**. Fees include processing, appraisal, legal and insurance costs and government levies. It defines **APR as "the numerical representation of the Total Cost of Credit"** ([costofcredit.co.ke glossary](https://costofcredit.co.ke/site/glossary)). The site calls its APRs "indicative" and publishes no annualisation formula. Its glossary still mentions the retired KBRR, so the page has not been fully updated. The legal basis is Banking Act s.31A, "Disclosure of information on loans", together with CBK/PG/22 ([Banking Act](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/1989/9/eng@2024-12-27/source)). Neither text was read this session, because the CBK host is blocked. For digital lenders, the **DCP Regulations 2022 (LN 46/2022)** require disclosure before disbursement of every fee, the interest rate and whether it is on a reducing balance, and the **TCC (principal + interest + fees)** ([money254](https://www.money254.co.ke/post/how-cbk-regulation-affect-borrowers-of-digital-loans-personal-loans); [Kenya Law LN 46/2022](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/2022/46/eng@2022-04-22)). No source found requires an APR for digital lenders. CBK's March 2026 consumer-protection framework is still a draft ([CBK](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Consumer-Protection-Framework-March-2026.pdf)).

So the honest presentation has two figures, and only one of them is regulated. The **primary figure is TCC in shillings, for the user's own amount and actual holding period**. That is the concept both rule sets use, and it is the only figure that does not mislead someone with a one-off 30-day need. The **secondary figure is an APR computed as the internal rate of return on net cash received, compounded**. It should sit beside a separately labelled nominal (× 365 / days) figure, because Kenyan lenders print the uncompounded version. If a single "APR" column mixes the two, digital credit looks cheaper than it is next to a compounded bank figure.

A 30-day product does not belong in a 36-month column. **It should be shown for its real term.** A rolled-over scenario can sit beside it, but only when the rollover rules are modelled explicitly. M-Shwari rolls a balance unpaid on **day 31** into a new 30-day period, charging **another 7.5% plus excise on the unpaid principal** ([NCBA Key Facts](https://ke.ncbagroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/M-Shwari-Key-Facts-21-June-23.pdf)). How many rollovers are allowed before default is not established. Tala does not roll over at all. It ends at day 61 with an 8% charge. Fuliza's daily fee "runs until the balance is cleared", and the commonly cited 30-day limit was not confirmed. Finally, s.44A in duplum, which caps the interest recoverable on a non-performing loan at the principal, matters only for a default scenario, and it applies to institutions under the Banking Act ([TripleOKLaw](https://www.tripleoklaw.com/?p=7195)).

### Defects in JiPange `lib/loan-comparison.ts`

| Defect | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| The `apr` column mixes five conventions. SACCO is compounded effective (12.68%), bank is nominal (15%), the digital lender uses a flat-to-reducing approximation, Fuliza uses simple annualisation, and M-Shwari is compounded `(1.075)^12 − 1` = 138%. | One column needs one stated convention, ideally compounded IRR on net cash, with nominal shown separately. |
| The digital-lender row charges 15% a month flat × `termMonths`, for any term up to `MAX_TERM_MONTHS`. | Tala's longest term is 61 days, priced per day used. A 12-month or 36-month digital-lender loan at that price does not exist as modelled. |
| The M-Shwari row leaves out the 1.5% excise, and treats the cost as `7.5% × termMonths` on gross principal. | The real cost is 9% of principal on 98.5% received: 9.14% per 30 days, not 7.5%. Multi-month cost needs the day-31 rollover rule, which applies to unpaid principal only. |
| Fuliza is held for `termMonths × 30` days. | The fee runs until the balance is cleared, but no source confirms that Fuliza can run for months. Mark any figure past 30 days as hypothetical. |
| `fuliza.ts` applies 20% excise to the 1% access fee. | Excise on the daily fee is supported. Excise on the access fee is not established. |
| SACCO and bank rows carry no fees, but sit in a column labelled like an APR. | TCC by definition includes processing, insurance and legal fees. Without fees, the rows show a nominal rate, not a cost of credit. |
| The SACCO row is reducing-only. | Flat-rate SACCO quoting is common, and it roughly doubles the effective rate. |

## CBK prices bonds on an exact 364/182 grid that Mwangaza already reproduces

CBK's FAQ gives the price as the present value of the remaining semi-annual coupons and principal, discounted at r = yield / 2. It also states that **"the Day Count Convention for pricing Treasury bonds in Kenya is 364 days"** ([CBK FAQ, 2014](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/images/FAQs/FAQsGoKSecurities.pdf)). The FAQ is twelve years old and no current document restates the formula. Current prospectuses, however, print enough figures to test it. The exact engine is **dirty = Σ CF / (1 + y/200)^(days to the cash flow / 182)**, **accrued interest = coupon × days since the last coupon / 364**, and **clean = dirty − accrued interest**. It reproduced the accrued interest for IFB1/2021/021 (12.737 × 161/364 = **5.6337**) and the matching dirty price of **105.6134** at 12.7370%. CBK prints both in the prospectus's own worked example: "dirty price is the clean price (KES 99.9797) plus AI (KES 5.6337)" ([CBK Aug 2026 IFB prospectus](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/618766331_AUGUST%202026%20IFB1-2019-016,%20IFB1-2021-018%20AND%20IFB1-2021-021%20DATED%20%2017-08-2026--.pdf)). It also reproduced the pricing-table rows in the 2025 and 2024 prospectuses ([CBK Aug 2025](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/654434758_AUGUST%202025%20IFB1-2018-15%20AND%20IFB1-2022-019%20DATED%20%2018-08-2025.pdf); [CBK Aug 2024](https://centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/718912010_AUG%202024%20IFB1-2023-6.5%20AND%20IFB1-2023-017%20DATED%2019-AUG%202024.pdf)), along with the FXD4/2019/010 switch example (clean 99.9951, AI 5.9713, dirty 105.9664) ([CBK Nov 2026 switch prospectus](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/1533456351_NOVEMBER%202026%20SWITCH%20FROM%20FXD1-2023-005,%202696-091,%202657-182,%202585-364%20TO%20FXD4-2019-010%20DATED%2011-11-2026%20REVISED.pdf)). Dividing by 365 fails every case. Coupon dates step **exactly 182 days**, ending on maturity. They are not the same calendar day each half-year: for example, 7 Sept 2026, 8 Mar 2027, 6 Sept 2027. A full period therefore accrues exactly c/2, which makes the /364 accrual and the /182 exponent a single convention rather than two separate choices. `src/lib/constants.ts`, `financial-engine.ts` and `sell.ts` all encode this already.

"Price per 100" means different things depending on where it is printed. **Pricing tables show clean prices. Switch prospectuses show dirty prices** (the repository's `switch.ts` established this: 115.8913 reproduces 9.9627% only when read as dirty). **The cash that settles is dirty.** CBK says withholding tax is computed on the clean price ([CBK FAQ](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/images/FAQs/FAQsGoKSecurities.pdf)); the rates themselves are in the earlier tax report. T-bills follow a separate rule, P = 100 / (1 + r·d/36500), on a **365-day** basis. The FAQ's 98.128 example reproduces only on 365, and `src/lib/tbills.ts` already uses it.

On the NSE secondary market, settlement is delivery-versus-payment at CBK, on a value date the counterparties choose **from T+0 to T+3** ([CBK settlement guidelines, Jul 2023](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Operational-Guidelines-for-Automated-Clearing-and-Settlement-for-Government-of-Kenya-Treasury-Bonds-July-2023.pdf)). Clearstream says T+3 "is required" ([Clearstream](https://www.clearstream.com/clearstream-en/res-library/market-coverage/securities-trading-kenya-5116314)), a wording that sits uneasily with CBK's "may choose". Bonds trade in **multiples of KSh 50,000**. **The register closes two working days before a coupon or redemption date**, and bonds cannot trade in the last three working days before principal redemption. The finding that matters for reconciliation is this: **NSE's 5 February 2026 dirty prices reproduce CBK's formula only when valued at the trade date.** Seven bonds matched to 4 decimals at T+0, including three amortising IFBs. None matched at T+1 or T+3 ([NSE BondPrices 05-Feb-2026](https://www.nse.co.ke/wp-content/uploads/BondPrices_05-FEB-2026.pdf)). One IFB1/2018/015 row pairs a T+0 dirty price with a T+1 accrual, so the sheet's clean column should be treated as derived and occasionally inconsistent. The NSE sheet was used only to check the formula; repository policy bars redistributing its data.

## Amortising IFBs redeem a share of what is left, and price per 100 of it

CBK writes each schedule as dated percentages "of unencumbered outstanding principal amount". IFB1/2023/6.5, for example, reads: "10 May 2027-50% amortization on outstanding principal amount, 7th May 2029-30% of unencumbered outstanding principal amounts and 6th May 2030-100%" ([CBK Aug 2024](https://centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/718912010_AUG%202024%20IFB1-2023-6.5%20AND%20IFB1-2023-017%20DATED%2019-AUG%202024.pdf)). Numerical tests settle three rules. **First, the fraction applies to the balance outstanding.** At 17.00%, CBK prints **102.5044**. Reading 30% as 30% of the remaining 50 gives 102.5044. Reading it as 30 per 100 of original face gives 102.4471, and a bullet model gives 103.2417. **Second, coupons after a redemption are paid on the reduced balance.** For IFB1/2021/018 at 11%, the amortised model gives 108.0281 against CBK's 108.0281, and a bullet model gives 111.2039 ([CBK Aug 2026](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/618766331_AUGUST%202026%20IFB1-2019-016,%20IFB1-2021-018%20AND%20IFB1-2021-021%20DATED%20%2017-08-2026--.pdf)). **Third, prices are per 100 of the face outstanding at settlement.** IFB1/2018/015's 2025 table reproduces only when its balance is re-based to 100 after its earlier steps ([CBK Aug 2025](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/654434758_AUGUST%202025%20IFB1-2018-15%20AND%20IFB1-2022-019%20DATED%20%2018-08-2025.pdf)). The NSE market applies amortisation the same way. Schedules vary from issue to issue (50/50; 40% of outstanding; 50% then 30% of outstanding), so they must come from each issue's own prospectus. They cannot be inferred from the issue code.

"Coupon accrual across off-cycle repayments" is listed as P0 in the 13 November audit. On the evidence, **it is probably moot**. **Every amortisation date in the current prospectuses falls exactly on a coupon date** of the bond's 182-day grid: 14 Oct 2030, 1 Apr 2030, 1 Sep 2031, 17 Jan 2028, 9 Feb 2032, 10 May 2027, 7 May 2029 and 28 Feb 2033 are all in CBK's printed coupon lists. The repository's own broker fixture (IFB1/2022/018, 2 June 2031) is on its grid too. No CBK document describes accrual for an off-cycle repayment, because CBK schedules none. An off-cycle date is far more likely to be a user's calendar-month approximation than a real schedule. The engine should reject it, or flag it as needing a prospectus citation, rather than invent an accrual rule.

**Holder eligibility makes some cash flows depend on the holder.** The 2025 IFB prospectus adds that **"any amounts up to Kshs 1.0 million per CSD account at amortization will be redeemed in full"** ([CBK Aug 2025](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/654434758_AUGUST%202025%20IFB1-2018-15%20AND%20IFB1-2022-019%20DATED%20%2018-08-2025.pdf)). A retail holding of IFB1/2018/015 or IFB1/2022/019 therefore behaves like a bond that matures on its first amortisation date, at a yield the pricing table does not show. The clause was not found in the 2024 or 2026 prospectus text. How CBK treats a holding just above KSh 1m is not stated. Primary-market limits are now **KSh 50,000 minimum and KSh 50 million maximum non-competitive per CSD account per tenor**, and **KSh 2 million minimum competitive** ([CBK Aug 2026](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/treasury_bonds_prospectuses/618766331_AUGUST%202026%20IFB1-2019-016,%20IFB1-2021-018%20AND%20IFB1-2021-021%20DATED%20%2017-08-2026--.pdf)). The 2024 cap excludes state corporations, public universities and semi-autonomous government agencies. The FAQ's KSh 20m non-competitive and KSh 100,000 IFB minimum are superseded. Non-competitive bids receive the weighted average rate. CBK rediscounts only "as a last resort, at **3% above the prevailing market yield or coupon rate whichever is higher**". A pledge not cancelled five days before amortisation sends the redeemed cash to the lender.

**Reconciling a broker quote** follows from all of the above. Brokers and the NSE Quotations Board quote **yields** ([NSE guidelines](https://www.nse.co.ke/wp-content/uploads/NSE-OPERATIONAL-GUIDELINES-FOR-THE-BOND-QUOTATIONS-BOARD.pdf)). Price the quoted yield with the amortised schedule at the agreed value date, then compare the result with the broker's price. A residual equal to the accrued interest means the clean and dirty labels are swapped. A residual of about n × coupon / 364 means the two sides valued on dates n days apart; for an NSE T+0 sheet against a T+3 value date, that is about 0.10 per 100 on a 12.5% coupon. A quote that reconciles only under a bullet model means the counterparty ignored amortisation, and CBK's convention wins. **Commission sits outside the price.** CBK's settlement amount is net, "commissions outside of this arrangement" ([CBK settlement guidelines](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Operational-Guidelines-for-Automated-Clearing-and-Settlement-for-Government-of-Kenya-Treasury-Bonds-July-2023.pdf)). The effective yield therefore comes from the all-in cash.

## Conclusion

The two engines fail in opposite ways. JiPange has approximately the right prices but a column that cannot be read. It compares a compounded 12.68% with a nominal 15% and a ×365 Fuliza rate, and asks a reader to rank them. Mwangaza has a formula verified to four decimals but no data for it to use on the bonds where it matters most. Both repairs are therefore mostly a matter of convention and data, not of research. For JiPange: pick one annualisation, lead with shillings, and stop stretching 30-day products across years. For Mwangaza: re-base amortisation to outstanding, and enter each IFB's schedule with its prospectus. One P0 item can probably be closed: off-cycle accrual answers a question CBK never asks.

Five facts remain open, and each should be shown in the UI as an explicit assumption rather than resolved by guessing: whether Fuliza's access fee attracts excise; how many M-Shwari rollovers are allowed before default; how CBK handles ex-coupon trades in the closure window; the KSh 1m clause's threshold behaviour and whether it applies to 2024 and 2026 issues; and the exact APR formula CBK and KBA use. The NSE Fixed Income Trading Rules (2024) and the text of Banking Act s.31A and LN 46/2022 are the documents that would settle most of them. Each is a single supplied PDF, not a research project.
